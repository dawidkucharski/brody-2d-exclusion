\title{Paired spatial statistics under alternative embeddings: exact graph-count variance and a prime-pattern application}

\author[put]{Dawid Kucharski\corref{cor1}}
\ead{dawid.kucharski@put.poznan.pl}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}
\affiliation[put]{organisation={Poznan University of Technology},
            city={Poznan},
            country={Poland}}

\begin{abstract}
Comparing spatial summaries across alternative mappings of the same set of
labels requires inference that respects the dependence induced by the shared
labels. A paired random-labelling procedure is developed in which every
simulated label set is mapped through each candidate embedding, so that
contrasts are formed within realisations. For counts of occupied edges on
finite simple graphs, an exact finite-population variance is derived for the
difference between two normalised counts under uniform fixed-count sampling.
The variance depends on the two graphs only through two discrepancies: an
inverse-size-weighted measure of shared and unshared edges, and the squared
difference of their normalised degree sequences; a simpler expression obtains
when both graphs are regular. The formula is verified by exhaustive
enumeration on small graphs and by paired simulation. The procedure is
illustrated with three bijective embeddings of the primes not exceeding
$99\,856$, whose nearest-neighbour summaries differ substantially under the
fixed-count null, and with an open-data application in which the same
seismic-event cells are compared under rook and queen adjacency. All
conclusions are conditional on the stated null models and finite windows; no
asymptotic claim about the primes is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%=====================================================================

Spatial summaries of a finite sequence are affected by the map that assigns
its labels to locations. Prime numbers provide a concrete example: their
two-dimensional spiral representation has long been used to display
arithmetic regularities \citep{ULAM-SPIRAL-1963}. Prime adjacency in the
Ulam spiral was studied explicitly by \citet{JARA-VERA-SANCHEZ-AVILA-2021},
so spatial adjacency is not a new question in itself. At a different scale,
\citet{TORQUATO-ZHANG-DECOURCY-2019} analysed one-dimensional prime pair
correlations and a structure factor, with limiting conclusions conditional on
the Hardy--Littlewood conjecture. These results motivate, but do not determine,
the behaviour of the finite lattice embeddings compared here.

The statistical question addressed in this paper is how summaries of one
fixed set of labels change when the same labels are placed by different
bijective maps. In particular, a comparison is required to preserve the
dependence between embeddings: separate random point patterns would not
represent repeated mappings of the same selected labels. This dependence is
made explicit by mapping each random-label realisation through every embedding
and calculating contrasts within that realisation.

The Brody distribution,
\begin{equation}
P_\beta(s)=(\beta+1)as^\beta e^{-as^{\beta+1}},
\qquad
a=\left[\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+1}\right)\right]^{\beta+1},
\label{eq:brody}
\end{equation}
was introduced in quantum chaos as a one-parameter interpolation between the
Poisson and Wigner limits of one-dimensional level-spacing
statistics~\citep{BRODY-1981}.

Nearest-neighbour spacing fits have also been considered for prime numbers by
\citet{WOLF-2014}. For spatial point patterns, \citet{SAKHR-NIEMINEN-2005}
examined Brody-type fits for random points on fractals. Here $\beta$ is used
only as an empirical descriptor of the fitted spacing distribution, not as
evidence of a random-matrix symmetry class or a universal exclusion scale.

The nearest-neighbour index of \citet{CLARK-EVANS-1954} and the second-order
summaries described by \citet{RIPLEY-1981} provide complementary views of a
spatial pattern. Monte Carlo testing of spatial patterns was developed by
\citet{BESAG-DIGGLE-1977}; conditional inference under fixed-count models goes
back at least to \citet{RIPLEY-1977-MODELLING}, and random-labelling nulls for
comparisons under a shared label set were discussed by
\citet{GOREAUD-PELISSIER-2003}; a recent review of point-process
goodness-of-fit tests is given by \citet{FEND-REDENBACH-2025}. Graph-edge
counts under permutation have a longer methodological history: join-count
moments under randomisation were derived by \citet{MORAN-1948}, the
between-sample edge count of an arbitrary graph by
\citet{FRIEDMAN-RAFSKY-1979}, and graph-based two-sample tests were developed
by \citet{HENZE-1988}, \citet{SCHILLING-1986},
\citet{MAA-PEARL-BARTOSZYNSKI-1996} and \citet{CHEN-FRIEDMAN-2017}, building on
the finite-population inclusion probabilities of
\citet{HORVITZ-THOMPSON-1952}. The paired graph-count variance derived below
is a specific finite-population consequence of this random-labelling
framework, rather than a new general theory of graph permutation tests.

For unit-mean nearest-neighbour spacings under complete spatial randomness in
two dimensions, the analytic Rayleigh law,
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm CSR}(s)=\frac{\pi s}{2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi s^2}{4}\right),
\label{eq:rayleigh}
\end{equation}
coincides algebraically with the Brody family at $\beta=1$. The continuous CSR
law is, however, not the empirical null for binary fields on a lattice: the
density-matched lattice Bernoulli null fitted with the same procedure below
yields $\beta\approx1.47$ at the native density, because lattice discreteness
modifies the spacing distribution. The continuous and lattice reference
levels are therefore distinguished when they are reported.

Random-labelling and permutation tests for spatial point patterns are long
established, and exact permutation moments for edge counts under a
randomisation null date at least to the join-count results of Moran and the
general-graph treatment of Friedman and Rafsky. Comparing the same labelled
set across two embeddings, however, couples two such statistics through one
shared realisation, and the two graphs involved may have different, irregular
edge sets. A closed form for the variance of the difference between two
normalised counts in this paired setting does not appear to have been
recorded.

This paper provides that missing finite-population piece. For two simple
graphs on a common vertex set, the randomisation variance of the normalised
count difference is expressed through an edge-overlap term and a
vertex-degree-discrepancy term. The expression is exact, non-negative in its
components, and reduces to the regular toroidal case used for lattice shells.
The formula and a paired simulation procedure are illustrated with three
embeddings of the same finite prime set; the prime example demonstrates the
procedure rather than providing its justification. A further illustration
(Section~4) applies the formula to seismic-event cells under two nested
adjacency graphs on one cell population, the applied setting the method is
intended for.

The methodological contribution is an exact finite-population variance for the
difference of two normalised graph-edge counts under uniform fixed-count
random labelling, expressed through two graph discrepancies, together with a
paired simulation procedure that preserves the dependence between embeddings.
It is an extension of established permutation-moment results, not a new
general theory of spatial statistics.

\section{Methods}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Prime population and observation window}

One common integer population is used throughout. Let $W=316$ and
$N_{\rm pop}=W^2=99\,856$. The prime population consists of all primes
$p\le 99\,856$; there are $9\,580$ of them, giving a fill fraction
$\rho=9\,580/99\,856=0.09594$. The twelve primes in $(99\,856,10^5]$ are
excluded so that all three embeddings act on the same finite set in the same
square observation window $\{0,\dots,315\}^2$, and every descriptor is computed
for exactly this population.

\subsection{Deterministic embeddings}

Lattice indices $(x,y)$ are zero-indexed, with $x$ increasing across columns
and $y$ increasing down rows; the metric is Euclidean distance between cell
centres.

\begin{definition}[Row-major embedding]
The integer $n\in\{1,\dots,N_{\rm pop}\}$ is mapped to cell
$(x,y)=((n-1)\bmod W,\lfloor(n-1)/W\rfloor)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ulam spiral on an even side]
The spiral is generated on the $316\times316$ lattice with start cell
$(x_0,y_0)=(157,158)$ and directions east, north, west, south with step
lengths $1,1,2,2,3,3,\dots$; the $n$-th cell visited receives the integer $n$.
This procedure is a bijection of $\{1,\dots,99\,856\}$ onto
$\{0,\dots,315\}^2$ (verified computationally).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Finite Cantor-style diagonal sweep]
For $s=0,1,\dots,2W-2$, the cells $(x,y)$ with $x+y=s$ are visited in
increasing order of $y$, where $y$ runs from $y_0=\max(0,s-W+1)$ to
$y_1=\min(s,W-1)$ and $x=s-y$; the $n$-th visited cell receives the integer
$n$. Each cell of $\{0,\dots,W-1\}^2$ is visited exactly once (verified
computationally), so this map is a bijection of $\{1,\dots,W^2\}$ onto the
window. Consecutive integers occupy diagonal neighbours $(x,y)\to(x-1,y+1)$
within each diagonal, and the last cell of each diagonal is connected to the
first cell of the next diagonal by a jump whose length depends on $s$. This
finite-square sweep is not the unrestricted Cantor pairing function.
\end{definition}

The three mappings differ in which integer labels become lattice neighbours.
Row-major maps consecutive integers to horizontal neighbours except at row
transitions; the Ulam spiral maps consecutive integers to adjacent cells along
the spiral arm; the diagonal sweep maps consecutive integers to diagonal
neighbours within each block, with jumps at block boundaries.

\subsection{Nearest-neighbour descriptor}

Occupied cells are represented by their centres $(x+1/2,y+1/2)$ in
$[0,W]^2$. For each reference point, the distance to the nearest other
occupied point is calculated and divided by the mean distance for that
pattern. The sum of continuous Brody log-densities in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:brody} is maximised with $0\le\beta\le15$. Because lattice
distances have ties and are dependent, this is a quasi-likelihood fit, not a
joint likelihood for independent continuous observations. Fit discrepancy is
summarised by $D_{\rm fit}=\sup_s|\widehat F(s)-F_{\hat\beta}(s)|$, where
$\widehat F$ is the empirical spacing CDF and
$F_{\hat\beta}(s)=1-\exp(-as^{\hat\beta+1})$ is the fitted continuous CDF.
No independent-sample goodness-of-fit $p$-value is assigned to this statistic.
Three boundary treatments are considered: open Euclidean
distances; toroidal distances; and open distances from reference points at
least five lattice units from the boundary to all occupied points.

For each observed fit, $2\,000$ independent resamples of the empirical
distance histogram are refitted after each resample is normalised by its own
mean. Their 2.5th--97.5th percentiles are reported as descriptive sensitivity
ranges. Nearest-neighbour distances are dependent, so these ranges are not
claimed to have 95\% spatial confidence coverage.

\subsection{Null models and controls}

Let $M=W^2$ and let $N$ be the observed number of occupied labels. Under the
primary null, a set of exactly $N$ labels is sampled uniformly without
replacement from $\{1,\ldots,M\}$. This fixed-count random-labelling model is
conditional on $M$ and $N$; it is not asserted to be a generative model for
the primes. Each of the $B=4\,999$ independent random label sets is mapped
through all three embeddings. Consequently, the three statistics within each
realisation are paired. Equal marginal null distributions follow because each
embedding is a bijection, but the paired statistics need not be independent.

For embeddings $a,b$, let $D_{ab}=\hat\beta_a-\hat\beta_b$ and
$T=\max_{a<b}|D_{ab}|$. The global Monte Carlo value under the null is
$(1+\sum_{k=1}^{B}\mathbf{1}\{T_k\ge T_{\rm obs}\})/(B+1)$.
For each contrast, the same simulated maximum is compared with its observed
absolute difference. These single-step maximum-statistic values account for
the three displayed contrasts under the complete random-labelling null; no
general familywise claim is made for alternative null configurations. The
three maps and the window are treated as fixed in this exploratory comparison;
the test does not adjust for any prior selection among possible maps, windows,
or descriptors.

An inhomogeneous comparison uses independent label occupancies with
$p(1)=0$, $p(2)=1$, and $p(n)=c/\log n$ for $n\ge3$. The constant
$c=0.9961876724$ gives expected count $N=9\,580$; no probability is truncated
in this window. Each of $1\,000$ such label sets is again mapped through all
three embeddings. Its realised count varies, so it is a separate model, not a
fixed-count randomisation. The smooth intensity is motivated by the prime
number theorem and does not encode prime-pair constraints. Mapped
case--control-type comparisons of this kind follow the clustering tests of
\citet{CUZICK-EDWARDS-1990}.

For the row-major pattern, independent thinning is performed at target
densities $0.010$, $0.020$, $0.032$, and $0.050$ with $500$ masks per density.
The original density $9\,580/99\,856$ is shown once without thinning. At
each density, $1\,000$ independent Bernoulli lattice patterns provide a
comparison distribution. The thinned patterns share the same prime set and
are not independent realisations of the primes.

\subsection{Standard spatial diagnostics}

The Clark--Evans index is $R=2\sqrt{N/M}\,\bar r$, where $\bar r$ is the
open-boundary mean nearest-neighbour distance. Its conventional continuous
reference is not substituted for the simulated lattice reference.

For a toroidal distance shell $d$, each embedding $a$ induces an undirected
graph $G_{a,d}=(V,E_{a,d})$ on the integer labels $V=\{1,\ldots,M\}$.
An edge joins two labels when their mapped cells are separated by $d$ on the
torus. For the shells used here these graphs are simple and $m_d$-regular,
with $|E_{a,d}|=E_d=Mm_d/2$. Let $X_i$ indicate whether label $i$ is selected,
and write $(x)_k=x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1)$ and $p_k=(N)_k/(M)_k$. The occupied
pair count and its fixed-count normalisation are
\begin{equation}
C_a(d)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E_{a,d}}X_iX_j,
\qquad
g_a(d)=\frac{C_a(d)}{E_d p_2}.
\label{eq:g2exact}
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathbb E_0[g_a(d)]=1$ exactly for $N\ge2$. The shells
$d=1,\sqrt2,2,\sqrt5,4$ have $m_d=4,4,4,8,4$, respectively.

\subsection{Variance of a paired graph-count contrast}

The same construction can be used for any two nonempty simple graphs on
$M\ge4$ labels, including open-window shell graphs with nonconstant degrees.
Let $e_a=|E_a|$, $e_b=|E_b|$, $q=|E_a\cap E_b|$, and let $d_a(v)$ and
$d_b(v)$ be the degrees of label $v$ in the two graphs. Each occupied-edge
count is normalised by its own fixed-count expectation, $e_ap_2$ or $e_bp_2$.
Define the edge and degree discrepancies
\begin{equation}
S_e=\frac{1}{e_a}+\frac{1}{e_b}-\frac{2q}{e_ae_b},\qquad
S_v=\sum_{v=1}^{M}\left(\frac{d_a(v)}{e_a}-\frac{d_b(v)}{e_b}\right)^2.
\label{eq:graph-discrepancies}
\end{equation}
Under uniform fixed-count sampling, the difference of the normalised counts
has expectation zero under the null and exact null variance
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}_0(g_a-g_b)=
\frac{S_e(p_2-2p_3+p_4)+S_v(p_3-p_4)}{p_2^2}.
\label{eq:general-variance}
\end{equation}
The derivation is given in \ref{app:variance}. Unlike a periodic
regular graph, an open-window graph can have a nonzero degree-discrepancy
term $S_v$.

\begin{remark}[Absorption property]\label{rem:absorption}
Under the null, the variance in Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-variance} depends on the
two graphs only through $S_e$ and $S_v$: the between-graph degree interaction
$\sum_v d_a(v)d_b(v)$ is absorbed into these two discrepancies. Both
coefficients are non-negative, since
$p_2-2p_3+p_4=p_2\tfrac{(M-N)(M-N-1)}{(M-2)(M-3)}$ and
$p_3-p_4=p_3\tfrac{M-N}{M-3}$. Each shared edge carries the combined weight
$1/e_a-1/e_b$ in $S_e$, and $S_v=0$ if and only if the normalised degree
sequences coincide. For $N=2$ the variance reduces to $S_e/p_2$, and for
$N=M$ it is zero, as it must be. Equation~\eqref{eq:general-variance} is a
null variance: under non-uniform labelling it need not hold, and standardising
observed contrasts by it is then only descriptive.
\end{remark}

For two $m_d$-regular toroidal shells, $e_a=e_b=E_d$ and $S_v=0$. Writing
$q_d=|E_{a,d}\cap E_{b,d}|$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-variance} then gives
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}_0[C_a(d)-C_b(d)]
=2(E_d-q_d)p_2
\frac{(M-N)(M-N-1)}{(M-2)(M-3)}.
\label{eq:paired-variance}
\end{equation}
The variance of $g_a(d)-g_b(d)$ is Eq.~\eqref{eq:paired-variance} divided by
$(E_d p_2)^2$. Shell-null envelopes and the open-window variance check use
$2\,000$ of the paired fixed-count realisations.
Equation~\eqref{eq:general-variance} is a variance for normalised graph-edge
counts; it does not provide a variance for the nonlinear Brody fit of
Section~2.3, whose paired null distribution is obtained by simulation.

\subsection{Additional spatial summaries}

For comparison, a toroidal annulus summary counts all directed pairs with
$1\le d<3$ and divides by $N(N/M)\,8\pi$. The annulus contains 24 nonzero
lattice displacement vectors; its fixed-count expectation is therefore
$\frac{3}{\pi}\frac{M(N-1)}{N(M-1)}$, not exactly one. This summary uses all
occupied reference points.

Ripley's $K$ is estimated with the minus-sampling border correction. If
$I_r$ contains the $n_r$ points at least distance $r$ from the window
boundary, the estimator is
\begin{equation}
\widehat K_{\rm border}(r)=\frac{M}{N n_r}
\sum_{i\in I_r}\sum_{j\ne i}\mathbf{1}\{d_{ij}\le r\}.
\label{eq:border-k}
\end{equation}
$L(r)-r=\sqrt{\widehat K_{\rm border}(r)/\pi}-r$ is calculated at 18
geometric bin centres between 1 and $W/3$ lattice units and directly at
$\bar r$. A pointwise null envelope is based on $200$ fixed-count patterns;
it is not a simultaneous significance envelope
\citep{LOOSMORE-FORD-2006,MYLLYMAKI-2017}.

For box width $\varepsilon\in\{2,4,8,16,32,64,128\}$, the window is cropped
to complete boxes and $Z_2(\varepsilon)=\sum_k[M_k(\varepsilon)/M_{\varepsilon}]^2$
is formed from box masses $M_k$ and retained mass $M_{\varepsilon}$. The fitted
log--log slope is a finite-range summary. Separately, the pairwise correlation
sum of \citet{GRASSBERGER-PROCACCIA-1983},
\begin{equation}
C(r)=\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\sum_{i<j}\mathbf{1}\{d_{ij}\le r\},
\end{equation}
is evaluated at 12 geometrically spaced open-boundary radii from 2 to $W/3$.
Its log--log slope is reported without an edge correction. Because the window
and fitted ranges are finite and box cropping changes the support, neither
slope is interpreted as an asymptotic dimension.

\subsection{Computational reproducibility}

All quantities reported in Sections~3--5 are recomputable from the seeded
scripts in the accompanying package; the package records the random seeds,
simulation sizes and software versions. The random-labelling Monte Carlo
values are conditional on the fixed-count null, the selected maps and window,
and the chosen statistic; no correction is claimed for prior selection among
alternative analyses. The exact variance of Section~2.6 holds under the null;
outside the null it is used only descriptively. GitHub Copilot Chat for Visual
Studio Code (GitHub, extension version 0.72.0) assisted with development of
the analysis and plotting code; no generative image model was used to
fabricate or alter the plotted data.

\subsection{Embedding dependence of the local spacing fit}

The open-boundary estimates are $2.153$, $1.856$, and $1.413$ for row-major,
Ulam, and diagonal-sweep embeddings, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:local}).
Their empirical-CDF discrepancies are $0.267$, $0.315$, and $0.234$; the
largest tied-distance fractions are $0.320$, $0.594$, and $0.344$,
respectively. The continuous family is therefore not a close distributional
model for these discrete observations. The same fitting rule is nevertheless
applied to each observed and simulated pattern, so its paired contrasts remain
well-defined statistics under the specified random-labelling null.
Their marginal fixed-count null means are all approximately $1.467$, with
standard deviation approximately $0.020$. The observed pairwise differences
are $0.297$, $0.739$, and $0.442$. None of the $4\,999$ paired null maxima
exceeds even the smallest of these contrasts; the global and three
maximum-statistic Monte Carlo values are therefore $1/5\,000=0.0002$
(Table~\ref{tab:paired}). These values quantify disagreement with the
specified complete random-labelling null, not a probability that the observed
prime pattern arose randomly.

The same ordering is obtained under toroidal and interior-reference distances
(Table~\ref{tab:local} and Figure~\ref{fig:boundary-sensitivity}). The
interior-reference counts are $8\,894$, $9\,041$, and $8\,918$, respectively.
The resampling ranges in Table~\ref{tab:local} and
Figure~\ref{fig:embedding-robustness} describe sensitivity to resampling the
observed distance histogram and are not confidence intervals for a spatial
process.

\subsection{Mapped inhomogeneous control}

The mapped inhomogeneous model gives mean $\pm$ standard deviation
$1.450\pm0.022$, $1.441\pm0.021$, and $1.446\pm0.021$ for row-major, Ulam,
and diagonal-sweep embeddings. In this model, the differences between the
first and second and between the first and third means are $0.010$ and
$0.004$, respectively, much smaller than the corresponding observed
contrasts. Thus this particular independent-occupancy model with a smooth
prime-number-theorem intensity does not reproduce the observed ordering and
magnitude. Other inhomogeneous or interaction models are not excluded.

\subsection{Exact-distance pair correlations}

The normalised exact-shell counts show different local configurations across
the three embeddings (Table~\ref{tab:g2} and Figure~\ref{fig:g2-distance}).
For example, the Ulam shell at $d=\sqrt2$ has $g_a=2.058$, whereas the
corresponding row-major and diagonal-sweep values are $0.001$ and $1.008$;
at $d=2$ the Ulam value is $1.670$ and the diagonal-sweep value is $0.010$.
The shell values are
descriptive summaries of the same selected labels; they are not independent
observations and no simultaneous test across the five shells is claimed.

For the row-major embedding, the normalised horizontal and vertical counts
are $1.332$ and $1.337$ at distance 2, and $1.339$ and $1.405$ at distance 4;
the direction-dependent differences reflect the periodic wrap of the
horizontal toroidal shifts. The corresponding bounded twin- and cousin-prime
counts are compared with the asymptotic Hardy--Littlewood constant in
Appendix~\ref{app:supplementary}.

\subsection{Validation and power of the paired procedure}

For open-boundary $d=\sqrt2$ graphs, Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-variance} predicts
contrast standard deviations of $0.0299$--$0.0300$; the corresponding values
from $2\,000$ paired realisations are $0.0301$--$0.0306$
(Table~\ref{tab:open-variance}). Unlike the toroidal example, the normalised
degree-discrepancy terms are nonzero. The agreement checks the analytic
variance for this finite setting, rather than proving a distributional limit.

Calibration under planted structure uses the toroidal $d=\sqrt2$ shell, whose
two embedding graphs share no edges. At each enrichment strength
$\theta\in\{0.5,1,2\}$, $400$ label sets of size $9\,580$ were generated by
preferential selection: the weight of an unselected label was multiplied by
$\exp(\theta)$ once for each already selected label whose integer difference
from it lies in a designated gap set. Two alternatives were planted: the
row-major shell gap classes $\{315,317\}$, which carry $99.4\%$ of that
shell's edges, and the seven dominant Ulam shell classes
$1246,1248,\ldots,1258$, which carry only $2.2\%$ of theirs. The experiment is
a calibration of level and sensitivity under planted structure, not a
balanced comparison of the two embeddings, and it is not claimed to show that
the paired test dominates the marginal tests in general. With $2\,000$ null
label sets and fixed thresholds at $\pm1.96$, the paired rejection rate at
nominal $5\%$ is $0.046$ and the marginal rates are $0.055$ and $0.051$
(Table~\ref{tab:power}); no empirical critical value is estimated from these
null sets (Appendix~\ref{app:supplementary}). Under
row-major planting, the paired contrast and the marginal row-major test reject
at every strength, with mean standardised contrasts of $17.6$, $41.6$ and
$105.7$, while the marginal Ulam test stays at $0.053$, $0.063$ and $0.208$.
Under Ulam planting the contrast reverses sign, with mean standardised values
of $-0.48$, $-3.30$ and $-61.44$, and its rejection rate rises from $0.103$
to $0.995$; the marginal Ulam test responds earlier and equally or more
strongly, so the pairing provides no consistent gain in power in this
calibration. The sign of the paired contrast identifies the carrying embedding
in every row-major realisation and in $0.683\pm0.023$, $0.785\pm0.021$ and
$0.970\pm0.009$ of the Ulam realisations at $\theta=0.5$, $1.0$ and $2.0$
(proportions and Monte Carlo standard errors). The differing growth rates
reflect how concentrated the planted gap classes are in each shell, not an
intrinsic difference between the embeddings.
Figure~\ref{fig:power-calibration} displays the curves.

\section{Application: seismic-event cells under two adjacency graphs}
%=====================================================================

The graph form of the method is exercised on a conventional open dataset. The
Advanced National Seismic System Comprehensive Catalogue of the
U.S.~Geological Survey \citep{USGS-ANSS-COMCAT} supplies $37\,590$ earthquake
epicentres with magnitude at least $2.5$ in the California region
($124.5^\circ$--$114^\circ$~W, $32^\circ$--$42^\circ$~N) for 2000--2026. The
epicentres are rasterised onto a $96\times96$ cell grid, so the finite
population consists of $M=9\,216$ cells and the label set consists of the
$2\,920$ cells containing at least one epicentre. Two alternative
neighbourhood graphs are defined on this one population: rook adjacency
(cells sharing an edge; $18\,240$ edges) and queen adjacency (rook plus
diagonal cells; $27\,265$ edges). The rook edge set is a subset of the queen
edge set, so the graphs are nested, open-window and irregular.

The normalised occupied-edge counts are $2.020$ (rook) and $1.970$ (queen)
(Table~\ref{tab:seismic}). Their difference of $+0.0503$ equals $5.47$
standard deviations, where the exact standard deviation $\sigma=0.0092$ comes
from Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-variance}. The $1\,999$ paired fixed-count
realisations give a Monte Carlo standard deviation of $0.0092$ and no
realisation whose contrast exceeds the observed one in absolute value, so the
Monte Carlo value is $0.0005$. Because the graphs are nested, the
edge-discrepancy term carries $99.7\%$ of the exact null variance and the
degree-discrepancy term the remaining $0.3\%$. The positive contrast means
that the occupied cells are relatively more connected through shared edges
than through corner contacts, consistent with elongate clusters along the
roughly north--south trending fault system. The comparison illustrates how
the adjacency definition changes the normalised clustering summary of one
population; it is not presented as a seismological hypothesis test. The raster
resolution is $0.11^\circ\times0.10^\circ$, and catalogue completeness varies
in space and time, so the cell set is a descriptive discretisation.

\section{Discussion}
%=====================================================================

The paired construction separates two questions: how the same selected labels
behave under different maps, and how large such contrasts are under a specified
label-randomisation model. Using one simulated label set for every map
preserves their dependence. The exact graph-contrast variance adds an
analytical check that applies to both regular toroidal and irregular
open-window graphs. When the graphs have equal size and degree structure,
greater edge overlap decreases the variance; when the degree structures
differ, the discrepancy term contributes additionally. This dependence is
exact under the fixed-count null, and it provides a geometric explanation of
why some embedding contrasts are intrinsically more variable than others.

For the prime pattern, the three nearest-neighbour fits differ substantially
relative to the paired null, the ordering survives the boundary treatments
considered, and the mapped independent-occupancy model with the stated smooth
intensity does not reproduce the observed contrasts. Neither comparison shows
that the pattern would remain unusual under every plausible structured null.
The exact-shell counts provide a local view: each embedding makes different
integer-label pairs spatial neighbours, with periodic wrap contributing its
own pairs. Because the continuous Brody family fits the tied lattice
distances poorly, its parameter is reported only as a descriptive summary and
its resampling ranges are not confidence intervals.

These two strands address one methodological question. The paired simulation
compares the fitted spacing summaries of Section~2.3, for which no closed form
is available; Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-variance} supplies the exact null
variability of the linear graph-count summaries, whose weights are fixed by
the maps alone. Both describe how summaries of one labelled population change
under alternative embeddings; Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-variance} is not a
variance for the nonlinear Brody fit. The graph form is also the one with
direct statistical practice outside arithmetic examples: two alternative
neighbourhood graphs on one finite population arise whenever the same events
are analysed under competing adjacency definitions, such as rook and queen
contiguity for areal data, distance-band versus $k$-nearest-neighbour graphs
for point events, or two regional partitions of a single study region. In
such settings Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-variance} gives the exact null variance of
the contrast between the two graph encodings without simulation, and the
discrepancy terms identify whether the difference is driven by shared edges
or by the degree structure. Section~4 applies the construction to
seismic-event cells under two nested adjacency graphs, where the
shared-edge term dominates by construction.

The analysis has several limitations. The three embeddings and the primary
window were selected for this comparison, and no general ordering across
other maps or windows follows. The Brody family is only a quasi-likelihood
descriptor and its fitted continuous CDF differs substantially from the
observed tied-distance CDF; its distance-resampling ranges do not account for
spatial dependence. The
fixed-count null is exchangeable in the integer labels, whereas the prime
sequence is deterministic and highly structured. The mapped inhomogeneous
model addresses a smooth trend but retains independent occupancies. Finally,
the multiscale slopes depend on their finite support and fitted ranges.

%=====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%=====================================================================

Spatial summaries of one fixed label set can change substantially under
different bijective embeddings. A paired random-labelling comparison preserves
the dependence induced by the shared labels, and an exact variance for
normalised graph-count contrasts is determined by edge overlap and degree
differences alone. In the example considered, all three observed Brody
contrasts exceed the simulated maxima under the stated fixed-count null. The
inference is conditional on that null, the selected maps and the finite
window; it is not an asymptotic statement about primes, and no descriptor is
claimed to be universally superior. The seismic-cell application shows the
exact variance at work on a conventional open dataset, where two adjacency
definitions of one cell population give materially different normalised
clustering summaries.

\section{Variance of a paired graph-count contrast}
\label{app:variance}

Let $G_a$ and $G_b$ be nonempty simple graphs on the same $M\ge4$ labels, with
$e_a$ and $e_b$ edges. A set of $N$ labels is drawn uniformly without
replacement. For $e=\{i,j\}$, let $Y_e=X_iX_j$ and assign the weight
$w_e=\mathbf{1}\{e\in E_a\}/e_a-\mathbf{1}\{e\in E_b\}/e_b$. Then
$g_a-g_b=p_2^{-1}\sum_e w_eY_e$, $\sum_e w_e=0$, and its expectation is zero.

Let $S_e=\sum_e w_e^2$ and $S_v=\sum_v(\sum_{e\ni v}w_e)^2$, as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:graph-discrepancies}. For ordered pairs of distinct edges, the
sum of $w_ew_f$ over pairs sharing a vertex is $S_v-2S_e$. Since
$\sum_e w_e=0$, the corresponding sum over disjoint pairs is $S_e-S_v$.
Their joint inclusion probabilities are $p_3$ and $p_4$; identical edges have
inclusion probability $p_2$. Therefore
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Var}_0(g_a-g_b)
&=p_2^{-2}\{S_ep_2+(S_v-2S_e)p_3+(S_e-S_v)p_4\} \\
&=p_2^{-2}\{S_e(p_2-2p_3+p_4)+S_v(p_3-p_4)\},
\end{align}
which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-variance}. The result applies to irregular
graphs and unequal edge counts, but not unchanged to weighted pair estimators
whose normalisation or edge weights depend on the realised labels.

If both graphs are $m$-regular with $E=Mm/2$ edges and $q$ shared edges,
$S_v=0$ and $S_e=2(E-q)/E^2$. Multiplication by $(Ep_2)^2$ gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:paired-variance}. Exhaustive enumeration on $4\times4$ graphs
was used to check irregular graphs with unequal edge counts at several
occupancies, as well as two regular shells for $N=3$. The simulated toroidal
check appears in Table~\ref{tab:paired}; an open-boundary check with
nonzero $S_v$ is shown in Table~\ref{tab:open-variance}.

over the unselected labels, and the selection order is not additionally
randomised. The sign-identification rates quoted in Section~3.4 are the
proportions of realisations in which the paired contrast has the sign
corresponding to the planted embedding.
