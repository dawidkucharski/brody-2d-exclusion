\title{Embedding geometry controls local spacing statistics in two-dimensional binary prime patterns}

\author[put]{Dawid Kucharski\corref{cor1}}
\ead{dawid.kucharski@put.poznan.pl}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}
\affiliation[put]{organisation={Poznan University of Technology},
            city={Poznan},
            country={Poland}}

\begin{abstract}
Deterministic embeddings of one-dimensional arithmetic sequences into two
dimensions can create or suppress apparent spatial structure. This paper studies
binary 2D embeddings of the prime sequence at $N=10^5$ under three
deterministic mappings: row-major order, the Ulam spiral, and Cantor pairing.
I compare a local spacing descriptor derived from nearest-neighbour statistics
with multiscale descriptors based on the correlation dimension $D_2$ and
Banach-space invariants. The main result is a sharp contrast between descriptor
classes. The nearest-neighbour spacing fit is strongly embedding-dependent: the
fitted Brody parameter changes from $2.15$ for row-major embedding to $1.87$
for the Ulam spiral and $1.40$ for Cantor pairing, while a matched-density
Bernoulli control gives $1.47\pm0.02$. A sparse-integer control shows that the
elevated row-major value is not a trivial consequence of low fill fraction.
Density-thinning experiments further show that absolute Brody values change with
density, but the row-major prime embedding remains separated from the
density-matched null. In contrast, $D_2$ remains elevated relative to the
Bernoulli null across deterministic embeddings, indicating that multiscale
mass-distribution signatures of the prime sequence are more stable than local
spacing signatures. These results show that 2D spatial statistics of arithmetic
patterns are jointly determined by sequence structure and embedding geometry,
and that local nearest-neighbour summaries and multiscale descriptors should
not be interpreted interchangeably.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%=====================================================================

Two-dimensional visualisations of arithmetic sequences have long been used as
heuristic tools for detecting hidden regularities. The best known example is
the Ulam spiral, where primes cluster along visually prominent diagonals that
are absent in a random arrangement of occupied sites~\cite{ULAM-SPIRAL-1963}.
Such constructions raise a natural statistical question: when a one-dimensional
arithmetic sequence is embedded in two dimensions, which apparent spatial
features belong to the sequence itself, and which are created by the embedding?

This question is harder than it first appears because different descriptors
probe different spatial scales. Local spacing summaries focus on short-range
adjacency, while multiscale descriptors respond to mass distribution over many
box sizes simultaneously. A sequence may therefore appear highly structured
under one descriptor and only weakly structured under another. The prime
sequence provides a useful benchmark because it is deterministic, sparse, and
subject to well-known local arithmetic constraints such as the impossibility of
two consecutive integers both being prime for $n>2$~\cite{HARDY-WRIGHT}.

The Brody distribution,
\begin{equation}
P_\beta(s)=(\beta+1)as^\beta e^{-as^{\beta+1}},
\qquad
a=\left[\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+1}\right)\right]^{\beta+1},
\label{eq:brody}
\end{equation}
was introduced in quantum chaos as a one-parameter interpolation between the
Poisson and Wigner limits of one-dimensional level-spacing
statistics~\cite{BRODY-1981}. It has since been used as a convenient empirical
summary of local spacing regularity in several fields
~\cite{STOCKMANN-1999,ALHASSID-2000,WEAVER-1989,LALOUX-1999}. For spatial point
patterns, Sakhr and Nieminen showed that nearest-neighbour spacing statistics of
random points on fractals can be described by Brody-type laws, thereby linking
RMT-inspired spacing families to spatial data~\cite{SAKHR-NIEMINEN-2005}. In
the present paper, $\beta$ is used only as an \emph{empirical fit parameter}
for local nearest-neighbour spacings, not as a universal exclusion scale and
not as evidence of any random-matrix symmetry class.

For unit-mean nearest-neighbour spacings under complete spatial randomness in
two dimensions, the analytic Rayleigh law,
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm CSR}(s)=\frac{\pi s}{2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi s^2}{4}\right),
\label{eq:rayleigh}
\end{equation}
coincides algebraically with the Brody family at $\beta=1$. This removes any
need to reinterpret $\beta$ as a corrected CSR baseline in the arithmetic
setting. The useful question is instead narrower: can a fitted Brody parameter
serve as a compact local descriptor that is sensitive to embedding geometry,
and how does that sensitivity compare with multiscale descriptors such as the
correlation dimension $D_2$?

This paper addresses that question for binary 2D prime patterns. Three
deterministic embeddings are compared: row-major order, the Ulam spiral, and
Cantor pairing. Two classes of controls are used. First, a matched-density
Bernoulli null separates arithmetic structure from trivial occupancy effects.
Second, a sparse-integer control and density-thinning experiments test whether
the row-major prime signal is merely a consequence of low fill fraction. In
parallel, multiscale structure is quantified through $D_2$ and complementary
Banach-space invariants. The central result is that local spacing fits are
strongly embedding-dependent, whereas multiscale descriptors remain more stable
across deterministic embeddings. The same prime sequence therefore does not have
a unique 2D spatial signature independent of how it is embedded and how it is
measured.

\section{Methods}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Binary prime surfaces}

Let $p_1<p_2<\cdots<p_{\pi(N)}$ denote the primes not exceeding $N$. The
binary prime surface of side $W=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor$ is the Boolean matrix
\begin{equation}
Z_{ij}=\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } iW+j+1 \text{ is prime},\\
0 & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad 0\le i,j<W.
\label{eq:binary}
\end{equation}
At $N=10^5$, $W=316$ and the surface contains $9\,592$ occupied sites, with
fill fraction $\rho=0.0959$.

\subsection{Deterministic embeddings}

Three deterministic embeddings are studied.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Row-major}:
  $n \mapsto (\lfloor(n-1)/W\rfloor,(n-1)\bmod W)$.
  \item \textbf{Ulam spiral}: integers arranged on an expanding square spiral
  centred in the lattice, following the construction of
  Ulam~\cite{ULAM-SPIRAL-1963}.
  \item \textbf{Cantor pairing}: integer sites visited by a diagonal sweep,
  using the pairing-map geometry as a deterministic alternative ordering.
\end{enumerate}

The point of this comparison is not to privilege one embedding as canonical,
but to ask whether observed spatial statistics are robust under plausible
deterministic 1D-to-2D mappings.

\subsection{Null models and controls}

Two null-model families are used.

First, a matched-density \emph{Bernoulli null} places occupied sites
independently with probability $\rho$, providing the natural baseline for
binary spatial patterns at the same fill fraction.

Second, a \emph{sparse-integer control} draws $\pi(N)$ integers uniformly from
$[1,N]$ and embeds them in the same row-major geometry as the primes. This
tests whether any elevation in the local spacing descriptor is genuinely
arithmetic or merely a generic consequence of sparsity.

To assess density effects directly, the row-major prime surface was randomly
thinned to target fill fractions
$\rho\in\{0.010,0.020,0.032,0.050,0.096\}$, with 20 realisations per density.
At each density, the same descriptor was evaluated on a density-matched
Bernoulli ensemble.

\subsection{Local nearest-neighbour spacing descriptor}

Occupied pixels are treated as a 2D point pattern. Euclidean nearest-neighbour
distances are computed using a KD-tree and normalised to unit mean. The
empirical spacing sample is then fitted by maximum likelihood to the Brody
family in Eq.~\eqref{eq:brody}. Throughout this paper, the fitted value of
$\beta$ is interpreted only as a one-parameter summary of local spacing
regularity. It is not assigned any universal meaning beyond the data and null
models actually compared here.

For the binary prime surface at native density, the relevant null is not the
continuous 2D CSR law but the density-matched Bernoulli binary field. This is
why row-major primes are compared primarily against Bernoulli and sparse-integer
controls, and why density-thinning uses matched-density Bernoulli baselines at
each target $\rho$.

\subsection{Multiscale descriptors}

To contrast short-range and multiscale statistics, the correlation dimension
$D_2$ is estimated from box-counting partition sums following the usual
R\'enyi-dimension framework~\cite{RENYI-DIMENSIONS,HENTSCHEL-PROCACCIA-1983,
GRASSBERGER-PROCACCIA-1983}. For a surface with box masses $M_k(\varepsilon)$
and total mass $M$, the order-$q$ partition function is
\begin{equation}
Z_q(\varepsilon)=\sum_{k}
\left(\frac{M_k(\varepsilon)}{M}\right)^q,
\end{equation}
and the correlation dimension is the case $q=2$.

Complementary Banach-space descriptors, including the Sobolev seminorm
$\|\nabla Z\|_2$ and the total variation, are used as additional multiscale or
geometric summaries. These quantities are not intended to replace $D_2$; they
provide an independent check that the arithmetic patterns are not being captured
by the local spacing fit alone.

\section{Results}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Embedding dependence of local spacing statistics}

Table~\ref{tab:local} summarises the main local-spacing results. The fitted
Brody parameter is strongly embedding-dependent. At $N=10^5$, row-major primes
yield $\beta=2.15$, the Ulam spiral yields $\beta=1.87$, and Cantor pairing
yields $\beta=1.40$. The matched-density Bernoulli reference gives
$\beta_{\rm Bern}=1.47\pm0.02$.

The key qualitative fact is the ordering. Row-major and Ulam embeddings lie
substantially above the Bernoulli baseline, while Cantor pairing does not. The
same 1D prime sequence therefore produces distinct local 2D spacing signatures
under different deterministic embeddings.

\subsection{Sparse-integer and density-thinning controls}

The sparse-integer control shows that the row-major prime signal is not a trivial
artifact of low occupancy. Replacing the prime set by uniformly sampled random
integers at matched count collapses the fitted value from $2.15$ to
$1.47\pm0.02$, indistinguishable from the Bernoulli null. The local-spacing
signal in the row-major prime embedding is therefore genuinely arithmetic in the
sense that it depends on the specific prime set, not merely on the number of
occupied sites.

Density-thinning changes the absolute value of $\beta$ substantially. At
$\rho=0.032$, chosen to match the fill fraction of the interferometric case in
the broader project, the row-major prime embedding drops to $1.46\pm0.02$;
at $\rho=0.020$, it drops further to $1.30\pm0.05$. However, at each tested
density the prime surface remains separated from the density-matched Bernoulli
reference. Thus, local-spacing summaries are both embedding-dependent and
density-dependent, and these two effects must be disentangled before any
substantive interpretation is attempted.

\subsection{Comparison with standard spatial descriptors}

A natural criticism of any specialised statistic is that it may add nothing to
established spatial diagnostics. To address this, Table~\ref{tab:descriptors}
reports, for the three prime embeddings and the two null configurations, the
Brody parameter alongside the Clark--Evans nearest-neighbour index
$R=\bar r\,(2\sqrt{\rho})$ (with $\bar r$ the mean nearest-neighbour distance),
Ripley's $K$ function summarised both as $L(r)-r$ evaluated at the mean
nearest-neighbour scale and through its maximal deviation over
$r\in[1,W/3]$ pixels, the pair correlation function averaged over the annulus
$1\le r<3$ pixels, and the correlation dimension $D_2$. Estimator details
(border correction, toroidal pair counting, bootstrap uncertainties) are given
in the supplementary material. Null uncertainties are ensemble standard
deviations over 20 realisations.

Three observations follow. First, among the local descriptors the Brody
parameter has by far the largest dynamic range across embeddings: the row-major
value lies about 45 null standard deviations above the Bernoulli level while
the Cantor value lies slightly below it, a contrast that no other local
statistic reproduces. Second, descriptors computed from the same
nearest-neighbour sample need not agree. The Clark--Evans index orders the
embeddings as $\beta$ does but compresses the contrast and places the Cantor
pattern marginally \emph{above} the null, because it uses only the mean spacing
whereas $\beta$ responds to the full shape of the spacing distribution.
Third, Ripley's $K$ is informative only at short scales: $L(r)-r$ evaluated at
the mean nearest-neighbour scale reproduces the embedding ordering
($-0.89$, $-0.24$, $-0.18$ against null $-0.14$), whereas the maximal deviation
over the full range separates only the Cantor pattern, at about three null
standard deviations, because cumulative fluctuations at large $r$ dominate.

The pair correlation function adds a distinctly arithmetic detail. For the
row-major embedding, $g_2$ is enhanced in the annulus $1\le r<3$ ($1.06$
versus $0.95$ for the null), reflecting the twin- and cousin-prime excess at
integer separations $2$ and $4$, which the row-major map converts into direct
horizontal neighbours. The Ulam and Cantor geometries do not map consecutive
integers to adjacent row sites and show no such contact enhancement; Cantor
instead shows small-scale exclusion. The correlation dimension completes the
picture: it is the only descriptor tested that remains elevated relative to the
null for all three embeddings, by $+0.025$ to $+0.044$ (6--11 null standard
deviations).

The Brody fit is therefore not proposed as a replacement for standard spatial
statistics. It is a compact shape summary whose embedding sensitivity
complements mean-based (Clark--Evans) and scale-integrated (Ripley, $D_2$)
diagnostics, and it is the only local descriptor among those tested that
separates all three embeddings in both direction and magnitude.
Figure~\ref{fig:descriptor-benchmark} summarises the comparison.

\subsection{Multiscale descriptors are more stable across embeddings}

The multiscale picture is qualitatively different. In the supplementary
analysis, the excess correlation dimension $\Delta D_2$ remains positive for the
prime sequence across row-major, Ulam, and Cantor embeddings, ranging from
$+0.053$ to $+0.089$ relative to the matched-density Bernoulli null at
$N=5\times10^4$. Thus, while the local spacing fit can be erased by a
deterministic re-embedding (Cantor), the multiscale mass-distribution signal
persists.

For row-major embedding at $N=10^5$, the correlation dimension of the prime
surface is $D_2=1.687$, while random permutation of the 1D-to-2D mapping lowers
it to $1.642\pm0.003$, consistent with the Bernoulli null level. A controlled
ordering of synthetic binary patterns places the primes between clustered and
strongly anti-clustered surrogates,
\begin{equation}
D_2^{(\mathrm{c})}=1.504 < D_2^{(\mathrm{B})}=1.645 <
D_2^{(\mathrm{p})}=1.687 < D_2^{(\mathrm{r})}=1.797,
\end{equation}
where the superscripts denote the clustered, Bernoulli, prime, and repulsive
surrogates, respectively.
This confirms that the multiscale descriptor is sensitive to nontrivial spatial
structure, but in a way that is less fragile to embedding choice than the local
nearest-neighbour fit.

The same contrast is visible in the multifractal spectra and the Banach-space
fingerprints, which remain distinguishable from the Bernoulli null even when the
local spacing descriptor becomes ambiguous. In the present context, this is the
most useful conceptual takeaway: local spacing and multiscale descriptors answer
different questions, and only the former is strongly controlled by the precise
2D geometry used to realise the prime sequence.

\section{Discussion}
%=====================================================================

The original broad manuscript attempted to interpret the Brody parameter as a
cross-domain exclusion measure. The present, narrower analysis supports a more
limited conclusion. For arithmetic patterns, the fitted Brody parameter is best
viewed as an empirical summary of local nearest-neighbour regularity. It is
useful precisely because it changes under deterministic re-embeddings that alter
local adjacency relations, even when the underlying 1D sequence is held fixed.

This makes the Cantor result particularly informative. If one observed only the
row-major embedding, it would be easy to conclude that the prime sequence has a
strong and intrinsic 2D local-spacing signature. The Cantor map shows that this
is false. The observed short-range structure is a property of the pair
(sequence, embedding), not of the sequence alone.

At the same time, the $D_2$ and Banach-space analyses show that not all spatial
signatures are equally fragile. The persistence of positive $\Delta D_2$ across
deterministic embeddings indicates that multiscale structure is more stable than
local nearest-neighbour structure. This contrast resolves the tension between
visual prime patterns, local spacing fits, and multiscale statistics: they are
not redundant views of the same object. The descriptor comparison of
Table~\ref{tab:descriptors} sharpens this point within a single point pattern:
mean-based, shape-based, and scale-integrated diagnostics carry different
information, and the fitted Brody parameter is the most embedding-sensitive
local descriptor tested, while $D_2$ is the most stable.

Three limitations should be kept explicit. First, the Brody family is used here
only as an empirical one-parameter fit; no claim of universality is made.
Second, only three deterministic embeddings are tested, so the observed ordering
should not be read as exhaustive. Third, the multiscale descriptor comparison is
strongest for the prime-versus-Bernoulli distinction and still deserves fuller
formal treatment for larger families of embeddings.

Within these limits, the study yields one robust conclusion: statements about
2D spatial structure in arithmetic data depend on both the embedding and the
descriptor. Any claim about ``prime point processes'' in two dimensions should
therefore specify both.

%=====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%=====================================================================

Two-dimensional binary prime patterns do not possess a unique local spatial
signature independent of embedding. The fitted Brody parameter changes from
$2.15$ for row-major order to $1.87$ for the Ulam spiral and $1.40$ for Cantor
pairing, while a matched-density Bernoulli null yields $1.47\pm0.02$. The
sparse-integer control shows that the elevated row-major value is not explained
by sparsity alone, and density-thinning shows that absolute values are
density-dependent even when the prime signal remains separated from the null.

In contrast, multiscale descriptors such as $D_2$ remain elevated relative to
the Bernoulli null across deterministic embeddings. Local spacing summaries and
multiscale mass-distribution summaries therefore capture different aspects of the
same embedded arithmetic pattern. A systematic comparison with standard spatial
diagnostics (Table~\ref{tab:descriptors}) confirms that the Brody parameter is
the most embedding-sensitive local descriptor tested, while $D_2$ is the most
stable. The appropriate conclusion is not that one
descriptor is universally superior, but that 2D statistical characterisations of
arithmetic sequences are observation-dependent: they depend on what embedding is
used and on which scale-sensitive descriptor is applied.

\section*{Supplementary Information}

\subsection*{S1. Extended validation of multiscale descriptors}

This supplement collects the arithmetic-sequence results that support the main
text but are not required for the core embedding-dependence argument.

\textbf{S1.1 Embedding stability of $D_2$.}
The $D_2$ signal for binary primes persists across three deterministic 2D
embeddings (row-major, Ulam spiral, Cantor diagonal) with
$\Delta D_2 = +0.053$ to $+0.089$ relative to the matched-density Bernoulli
null at $N=5\times10^4$. Under random permutation of the 1D-to-2D mapping,
$D_2$ drops from $1.687$ to $1.642\pm0.003$, consistent with the Bernoulli null.

\textbf{S1.2 Controlled ordering.}
At matched prime density, a simple surrogate ordering is obtained:
$D_2(\text{block-clustered})=1.504 < D_2(\text{Bernoulli})=1.645 <
D_2(\text{primes})=1.687 < D_2(\text{repulsive})=1.797$.

\textbf{S1.3 Additional arithmetic sequences.}
Beyond primes, the same workflow was applied to twin primes, primes
$\equiv 1 \pmod 4$, primes $\equiv 3 \pmod 4$, sums of two squares,
square-free numbers, the M\"obius functions $\mu^\pm$, the Liouville function,
and selected Beatty sequences. The forest plot in Figure~\ref{fig:S1} summarises
the corresponding $\Delta D_2$ values after multiplicity correction.

\textbf{S1.4 Finite-size behaviour.}
For row-major prime embeddings, the fitted Brody parameter remains above the
matched Bernoulli null across $N\in[10^3,10^7]$, although its absolute value is
non-monotonic. The full $\beta(N)$ series is listed in Section~S3.

\textbf{S1.5 Estimators for the descriptor comparison.}
For Table~\ref{tab:descriptors}, the Brody parameter is fitted by maximum
likelihood on unit-mean nearest-neighbour spacings with 200 bootstrap
resamples. The Clark--Evans index is the ratio of the mean nearest-neighbour
distance to the CSR expectation $1/(2\sqrt{\rho})$. Ripley's $K$ uses the
isotropic border correction, with the correction weight of each point taken as
the fraction of the circle circumference inside the window (64 angular
samples), on a log-spaced radius grid of 18 bins; $L(r)-r$ at the mean
nearest-neighbour scale is obtained by interpolation. The pair correlation
contact value is estimated by toroidal pair counting over the annulus
$1\le r<3$ pixels with a random sample of 3000 reference points. The
correlation dimension $D_2$ is the least-squares slope of the log--log
box-counting partition function of order $q=2$ over box sizes $2$--$128$
pixels. Null uncertainties are standard deviations over 20 independent
realisations of the matched-density Bernoulli field and the sparse-integer
control.

\subsection*{S3. $\beta(N)$ convergence data}

For the row-major binary prime embedding, the fitted Brody parameter was
computed at twelve values of $N$:
$N=10^3$ ($\beta=2.20$),
$N=2\times10^3$ ($\beta=2.10$),
$N=5\times10^3$ ($\beta=1.69$),
$N=10^4$ ($\beta=1.97$),
$N=2\times10^4$ ($\beta=2.19$),
$N=5\times10^4$ ($\beta=1.94$),
$N=10^5$ ($\beta=2.15$),
$N=2\times10^5$ ($\beta=2.05$),
$N=5\times10^5$ ($\beta=1.78$),
$N=10^6$ ($\beta=1.75$),
$N=5\times10^6$ ($\beta=1.86$), and
$N=10^7$ ($\beta=1.26$).
The trend is clearly non-monotonic; the main robust feature is not convergence
to a universal value, but persistent separation from the corresponding null over
the accessible finite-size range.
