\opening{Dear Editor,}

Please consider the manuscript ``Paired spatial statistics under alternative
embeddings: exact graph-count variance and a prime-pattern application'' for
publication in \emph{Spatial Statistics}.

The paper develops paired inference for comparing spatial summaries when one
fixed set of labels is mapped to locations in different ways. The central
result is an exact finite-population variance for the difference between two
normalised graph-edge counts under uniform fixed-count random labelling,
expressed through an edge-overlap discrepancy and a degree-sequence
discrepancy. Unlike established join-count and graph-based two-sample
moments, which treat individual counts, the expression covers the paired
difference of two separately normalised counts on the same labelled
population, including irregular open-window graphs with unequal edge sets and
non-identical degree sequences; the regular toroidal case follows as a
special case. The derivation is verified by exhaustive enumeration on small
graphs and by paired simulation, and the complete analysis is reproducible
from a single archived release (GitHub repository
\url{https://github.com/dawidkucharski/brody-2d-exclusion}; Zenodo version
2.4.1, DOI \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24012387}, concept DOI
\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23940934}), with every number in the
manuscript checked against the recorded results by an automated 239-item
audit.

Two applications demonstrate the method. First, three bijective embeddings of
the same finite prime set are compared under a paired fixed-count null, with
$4\,999$ paired realisations and a global maximum-statistic Monte Carlo value
of $0.0002$; the nonlinear Brody descriptor is reported descriptively and is
kept distinct from the exact graph-count result. A power-calibration study
under planted gap-class enrichment, with fixed thresholds, Monte Carlo
standard-error bars and an independent second-seed level check, shows that
the paired procedure retains its nominal level and attributes planted
structure to the correct embedding. Second, an open-data application
rasterises $37\,590$ USGS earthquake epicentres onto a $96\times96$ cell grid
and compares rook with full queen adjacency on the same cell population; the
normalised contrast is $+0.0523$ with exact standard deviation $0.0113$ and
Monte Carlo value $0.0005$, and the interpretation is limited to the chosen
rasterisation and the stated reference model.

The manuscript includes the required declarations: no competing interests, a
funding statement (no specific grant), a CRediT authorship statement, a data
and code availability section, and a declaration of generative AI and
AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process. The work has
not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. The
author approves the submission and takes responsibility for the content.

Thank you for considering this work for \emph{Spatial Statistics}.
